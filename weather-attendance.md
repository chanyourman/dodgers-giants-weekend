## What temperature does to batted-ball distance

The physics (Prof. Alan Nathan, University of Illinois; Baseball Prospectus):

- Warmer air is **less dense**, so the ball meets less drag and carries farther.
- Rule of thumb: **~0.3 feet of extra carry per 1°F** on a typical home-run trajectory — about **3 feet per 10°F**.
- That translates to roughly **1% more home runs per 1°F** of warming.
- For comparison, **wind is the much bigger lever**: a 5 mph out-blowing wind adds ~18–20 feet of carry.

**Applied to this weekend:** game-time temps in the low-to-mid 60s run about 10–12°F cooler than a typical warm-weather MLB game (~75°F), meaning roughly **3–4 feet less carry** on deep fly balls. Stack that on top of Oracle Park's MLB-worst home-run suppression and this is a weekend for doubles into the gaps, not moonshots. Friday's 16 mph WNW wind is the wild card — Oracle's bowl architecture mostly neutralizes the marine wind, but gusts can still hold balls up in right-center.
